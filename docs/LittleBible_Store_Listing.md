## Who this listing is written for

The buyer is not the child. It is a **Christian parent** — most often the person doing
bedtime, often tired, often quietly unsure they know enough to explain the Bible properly.
Secondary buyers: grandparents, Sunday-school leaders, homeschooling parents.

The listing sells **relief and competence**, not features. Five angles do the work:

1. **It is an actual Bible, not a storybook.** The Little Bible Version renders Scripture
   verse by verse in words a child can hold, beside the King James text — with what the
   verse means, a prayer, and a question to ask. Competing apps retell stories; this one
   hands a child the Bible itself. **Lead with this.**
2. **In order, not a grab-bag.** Nearly every children's Bible app is a pile of favourites.
   A child meets David having never heard of sin. Ours is a curriculum — 80 stories where
   each builds on the last.
3. **It tells the parent what to say.** Every story and every chapter has a guide naming the
   one idea to land and the mistake children usually make. The strongest single converter.
4. **It grows with the child, and covers the siblings.** Three reading levels — 3–5, 6–8,
   9–12 — change the games and how much a child reads themselves. One purchase covers every
   child profile on the device, each at their own level. This is what makes it worth buying
   for a family rather than a child.
5. **Grace, not moral lessons.** "Be brave like David" is the reading we deliberately avoid.

Lead with #1 and #4 in short fields, #3 in long copy.

### Caption rules

- **One line, under nine words.** Two-line captions render below thumbnail legibility.
- **Benefits, not labels.** "A guide for every story" beats "Parent Guides".
- **Never repeat a caption** across screens.
- Front-load the meaningful word — thumbnails crop from the right.

### What to avoid

- **No stock photos of children.** Invites scrutiny in a kids listing and looks nothing
  like the product. Use your own artwork.
- **No "#1", "best", or competitor names.** Grounds for rejection.
- **No screen that doesn't exist.** Every frame must be real UI — Apple rejects composite
  marketing images that misrepresent the app.
- **No emoji in captions.** Reads cheap beside hand-painted art.

## Three decisions before you submit

**1. Apple Kids Category — now a genuine trade-off.** The category requires picking one age
band, and Apple's top band is **9–11**. A 3–12 app doesn't fit any single band, and choosing
"5 and under" would misrepresent it. Options: opt out and compete in Education/Reference on
the strength of the curriculum, or enter under **6–8** and accept that the 9–12 material is
undersold. I'd **opt out** — your differentiator is range, and the category forces you to
hide most of it. (This reverses what a 4–7 range would have implied.)

**2. Age rating.** Rate **4+** on Apple and **Everyone** on Play. Do not let the crucifixion
and grief stories push you to a higher rating — they sit behind a parent PIN, which is
exactly the mitigation both stores expect. Declare it accurately in the questionnaire.

**3. Google Play "Teacher Approved".** You can't apply; Google selects. A listing stating
age range, learning outcome and no-ads clearly is materially likelier to be picked up.
