# Little Bible: Complete Discipleship Curriculum (Ages 4–7)

## Purpose and design standard

Little Bible is a gospel-centred, evangelical curriculum that takes a child through the Bible's one story: God made his good world; people sinned; God promised a Rescuer; Jesus the King came, died, and rose; the Spirit forms the church; and Jesus will renew all things. The curriculum is intentionally chronological and cumulative. Each story teaches one new doctrinal idea while retrieving truths already learned. “Mastery” at this age means that a child can recognise the truth, say it simply, connect it to the Bible story, and make one fitting response—not that the child can explain adult theological vocabulary.

The design synthesises six useful emphases in the requested sources:

- [The Gospel Project](https://www.gospelproject.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/TheGospelProjectKids_PreviewGuide.pdf): a chronological Bible timeline in which every part belongs to the gospel and points to Christ.
- [RCCG Sunday School](https://rccgsundayschool.org.uk/manuals/) and [RCCG Open Heavens](https://rccgonline.org/open-heavens-for-teens-17-june-2024/): Scripture joined to prayer, character, obedience, Spirit-empowered living, mission, and concrete daily response. Little Bible retains that African evangelical practicality while making grace—not performance—the ground of belonging.
- [Awana Cubbies](https://www.awana.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Cubbies_AppleSeed_ScopeSequence.pdf) and [Sparks](https://www.awana.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Sparks.pdf): one memorable core truth, one short verse, deliberate review, and age-layered recall.
- [Andrew Wommack Ministries, Foundational Truths](https://www.awmi.net/bw-foundation) and its [children's resources](https://www.awmi.net/children/): God's good character, salvation by what Jesus has done, and Christian conduct flowing from a received identity in Christ. These emphases are used under the governing authority of Scripture and the curriculum's broadly evangelical doctrinal spine.
- [What's in the Bible?](https://www.philvischer.com/blank-2): joyful, child-accessible coverage of the entire Bible rather than disconnected famous scenes.
- The [Catechism for Young Children](https://gdsmedia.org/wp-content/content/2018/03/Catechism-for-Young-Children-English.pdf): a systematic audit of Creator, human purpose, fall, law, covenant of grace, Christ's person and work, repentance and faith, prayer, church, resurrection, judgment, and eternal life.

All memory lines below are short selections from the public-domain **World English Bible (WEB)**. An ellipsis marks an intentionally shortened verse; the Bible reference—not the excerpt—is the assigned memory verse. Every line is ten words or fewer, suitable for the whole 4–7 range. Older children should gradually learn the complete verse from the family's preferred translation.

## 1. Theological Spine

The following truths are the scope-and-sequence charter. Later truths may be previewed, but they are not formally introduced before their prerequisites.

1. **God and revelation:** God is the one eternal, holy, loving, truthful King. He made everything, made people in his image, speaks through the Bible, and deserves trust, love, worship, and obedience.
2. **Creation and human purpose:** God's world was good. Every person has God-given dignity and was made to know God, reflect him, love others, work, and care for creation.
3. **Sin and judgment:** Humans distrusted and disobeyed God. Sin corrupts hearts and relationships, brings just judgment and death, and cannot be repaired by human effort.
4. **Promise and covenant:** God graciously promised a serpent-crushing Rescuer. Through covenants with Noah, Abraham, Israel, and David, God revealed his faithful plan to bless the nations through one people and one King.
5. **Law, sacrifice, and kingdom:** God's law reveals his holy character, names sin, restrains evil, and teaches redeemed people how to love; it cannot save. Passover, priest, sacrifice, tabernacle, king, and prophet prepare children to understand Christ.
6. **Jesus Christ:** The eternal Son became truly human while remaining truly God. Jesus is the promised Messiah, perfect Israelite, compassionate King, authoritative Teacher, sinless substitute, risen Lord, and only Saviour.
7. **The gospel response:** Jesus bore sin's judgment, died, rose bodily, and reigns. God calls every child to turn from sin and trust Jesus. Salvation is God's gift, received by faith—not earned by goodness.
8. **Union, identity, and growth:** Everyone who trusts Christ is forgiven, adopted, made new, and secure in God's love. Obedience, prayer, forgiveness, courage, generosity, and justice are grateful fruit of grace, empowered by the Spirit—not ways to buy God's love.
9. **Spirit, church, and mission:** The Holy Spirit lives in believers, helps them understand and obey God's Word, produces Christlike fruit, and equips the diverse church family to worship, serve, and tell every people about Jesus.
10. **Last things and hope:** Death is an enemy, but believers are with Christ and await bodily resurrection. Jesus will return, judge justly, defeat evil, and renew creation. God's people will enjoy him forever; therefore they live with hope.

### Non-negotiable gospel guardrails

- Never say that a child becomes God's child by being kind, brave, obedient, baptised, or churchgoing. These are responses to grace; salvation is through trusting Christ.
- Never turn an Old Testament character into the hero. Show human weakness, God's covenant faithfulness, and the way the event prepares for Jesus.
- Never pressure a scripted “decision.” Clearly invite repentance and faith, then encourage the child to speak with a trusted Christian adult. Do not give false assurance based on repeating words.
- Treat difficult material truthfully but non-graphically. Name sin, judgment, death, and resurrection without frightening imagery, manipulation, or invented details.

## 3. World Groupings

Each World contains eight commissioning units. A recommended rhythm is **one story per week**, with six short engagements: hear, retell, wonder, practise the verse, respond, and review. This makes each World eight weeks—within the required 6–10 week range. “Age entry” is the earliest recommended starting age, not an age lock. A seven-year-old who is new to the Bible still begins at World 1 and may use the accelerated six-week option described below.

| World | Stories | Unifying theological theme | Capstone | Normal duration | Recommended entry |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|---|
| In the Beginning | 1–8 | The good Creator made us; sin spoiled fellowship; God promised rescue and preserved the world. | Noah's Rainbow Promise | 8 weeks | Age 4 |
| Promise Family | 9–16 | God creates a covenant family by promise and faith, not human merit. | Joseph Forgives His Family | 8 weeks | Age 4+ |
| God Rescues a People | 17–24 | The holy “I AM” redeems, covenants with, teaches, and lives among his people. | God Lives with His People | 8 weeks | Age 4½+ |
| A Land Needing a King | 25–32 | God's people need his presence, his word, and a faithful shepherd-king. | David the Shepherd Boy | 8 weeks | Age 5+ |
| Heroes of Faith | 33–40 | Every human leader fails; God faithfully promises the perfect King and new hearts. | The Prophets Promise New Hearts | 8 weeks | Age 5+ |
| Jesus Is Here | 41–48 | The promised divine-human King arrives and calls dependent children to follow him. | Jesus Loves Children | 8 weeks | Age 5+ |

### Pacing and review rules

1. **Standard route:** eight weeks per World, 80 weeks total. Each new session retrieves the previous story and its concept.
2. **Younger or language-learning route:** ten weeks per World. Add review weeks after stories 4 and 8; do not add new doctrine.
3. **Older beginner route:** six weeks per World. Cover stories 1–4 in weeks 1–3, stories 5–7 in weeks 4–5, and never compress the capstone; week 6 is capstone and cumulative review.
4. **Advancement check:** before unlocking the next World, invite—not grade—the child to retell the World with picture cards, match four key words to stories, say one memory line, answer “What does this show us about God?”, and name one response. Repeat play if connections are missing.
5. **Spiral review:** every story should visibly reuse a timeline, promise thread, kingdom thread, rescue/sacrifice thread, and presence thread. The app should periodically ask, “What earlier story does this remind you of?”

## 4. Pedagogical Notes per World

### World 1 — In the Beginning

- **Entry capacity:** The child thinks concretely, experiences the world as made things and relationships, and can distinguish kindness from harm, but may equate God with a powerful created being.
- **Exit capacity:** The child can say that the one good God made everything and every person; identify disobedience as sin; explain that sin spoiled fellowship; and recognise God's first rescue promise and preserving covenant.
- **Risk if skipped/reordered:** Sin and salvation lose their setting. The child may hear “Jesus saves” without knowing the Creator's goodness, human dignity, what went wrong, or why rescue is needed.

### World 2 — Promise Family

- **Entry capacity:** The child understands promises, family belonging, waiting, jealousy, and forgiveness in concrete terms.
- **Exit capacity:** The child can distinguish trusting a promise from earning a prize; trace Abraham–Isaac–Jacob–Joseph; and recognise provision, grace, providence, and forgiveness as acts of the faithful God.
- **Risk if skipped/reordered:** Covenant becomes an unexplained religious word and faith becomes either wishful thinking or good behaviour. Jesus' identity as Abraham's promised offspring loses meaning.

### World 3 — God Rescues a People

- **Entry capacity:** The child can understand slavery as cruel captivity, rescue as being brought out, and house rules as following belonging.
- **Exit capacity:** The child can recount the rescue pattern—bondage, lamb, deliverance, covenant, commands, presence—and say that obedience follows rescue rather than buying it.
- **Risk if skipped/reordered:** The cross may sound like arbitrary violence, while law may sound like a ladder to earn love. Passover, redemption, holiness, and God's presence remain disconnected.

### World 4 — A Land Needing a King

- **Entry capacity:** The child admires visible strength and heroes and can confuse courage with feeling unafraid.
- **Exit capacity:** The child can define courage as trusting God's presence, recognise repeated human unfaithfulness, value listening obedience, welcome outsiders, and explain why God's people needed his chosen king.
- **Risk if skipped/reordered:** Bible characters become self-help superheroes. Children may imitate bravery while missing weakness, grace, representation, and the need for Christ the King.

### World 5 — Heroes of Faith

- **Entry capacity:** The child can hold two truths about one person: David can trust God and still sin; leaders can be gifts and still fail.
- **Exit capacity:** The child can trace king, wisdom, prophet, exile, mercy for nations, and new-covenant promises to expectation of Jesus and a changed heart.
- **Risk if skipped/reordered:** “Heroes of Faith” collapses into moralism or nationalism. Jesus appears without the offices, promises, exile problem, and universal mercy that explain who he is.

### World 6 — Jesus Is Here

- **Entry capacity:** The child recognises accumulated promise symbols and can understand that one person may fulfil earlier expectations.
- **Exit capacity:** The child can say that Jesus is truly God and truly human, Father's beloved Son, promised King, sinless representative, and welcoming Lord who calls disciples.
- **Risk if skipped/reordered:** Jesus may become merely a baby, teacher, or friendly helper. Without incarnation, Trinity, sinlessness, and messianic fulfilment, the gospel's Saviour is not properly identified.

### World 7 — The Compassionate King

- **Entry capacity:** The child can follow short parables, distinguish a sign from what it points to, and relate fear, hunger, exclusion, and lostness to daily life.
- **Exit capacity:** The child can connect Jesus' signs and parables to authority, forgiveness, provision, boundary-crossing mercy, seeking grace, welcome, prayer, and shepherd care.
- **Risk if skipped/reordered:** Jesus' miracles become magic and parables become manners lessons. The child reaches the cross without personally knowing the character and kingdom of the one who dies.

### World 8 — Jesus Saves

- **Entry capacity:** The child understands substitution, Passover, covenant, sin, death, and resurrection from earlier concrete stories and can express trust and turning in simple language.
- **Exit capacity:** The child can narrate Passion Week; explain that Jesus willingly died in sinners' place and rose bodily; distinguish gift from wages; and respond with repentance, faith, gratitude, and open conversation with a trusted adult.
- **Risk if skipped/reordered:** An early cross lesson can produce confusion, fear, or a thin formula. Without the resurrection, salvation becomes only death; without grace, response becomes pressure or works-righteousness.

### World 9 — Spirit-Filled Family

- **Entry capacity:** The child can grasp that the risen Jesus reigns though unseen and that gifts can change both belonging and action.
- **Exit capacity:** The child can identify the Spirit's presence and work, describe the church's practices, locate identity “in Christ,” welcome ethnic difference, endure difficulty with joy, and seek the Spirit's fruit.
- **Risk if skipped/reordered:** Christianity becomes a private decision followed by self-powered niceness. Children miss church, Spirit, mission, new identity, diversity, and grace-driven growth.

### World 10 — The King Makes All Things New

- **Entry capacity:** The child can name complex feelings, tolerate unresolved story tension, understand “now and later,” and separate hope from pretending pain is absent.
- **Exit capacity:** The child can use truth and prayer in fear, anger, loneliness, unfairness, and grief; distinguish Christian hope from denial; and confess Jesus' return, resurrection, judgment, and renewed creation.
- **Risk if skipped/reordered:** Heaven becomes escape into clouds, death receives sentimental answers, justice becomes revenge, and present suffering seems to disprove God's goodness. Revelation must conclude—not precede—the gospel arc.

## 5. What Makes This Different from a Story Bible

A story Bible usually selects memorable episodes and makes each readable on its own. Little Bible deliberately does the opposite: no story floats. A rainbow retrieves judgment and grace; a provided ram prepares for Passover; Passover prepares for the cross; David's throne and the prophets identify Jesus; resurrection prepares for Spirit, church, mission, bodily hope, and new creation. Each unit has one new concept, a named prerequisite, a short Scripture memory anchor, cumulative retrieval, and a capstone that checks whether the child can connect the parts. Life issues are not detachable advice modules: jealousy is read through Cain and sin's desire, fear through God's presence, unfairness through providence and final justice, anger through new-creation community, and grief through Jesus' tears and resurrection.

A child who completes every World should **know** the Bible's Genesis-to-Revelation storyline, the basic attributes and triune identity of God, the purpose of human beings, the nature and result of sin, the sequence of biblical covenants, the roles of law and sacrifice, the person and work of Christ, the gospel response, the church and Spirit, and Christian resurrection hope. The child should be invited to **believe** that Scripture tells God's true saving story, that Jesus alone saves by grace, that all who trust him are forgiven and welcomed, and that God's promises are trustworthy in joy and pain. The child should be able to **do** more than answer trivia: retell the gospel in simple words; locate a story on the Bible timeline; connect an Old Testament promise or pattern to Jesus; pray using Jesus' pattern; confess sin and ask forgiveness; seek adult help; show neighbour-love across differences; participate in church; share the good news without coercion; and face fear, anger, loneliness, unfairness, and grief with truthful, Scripture-shaped hope.

## Commissioning standard for every future story

Every commissioned script, activity, quiz, and illustration should pass these checks:

1. **Text fidelity:** Is the main claim stated by the cited passage? Are invented dialogue and details clearly avoided?
2. **One-new-concept rule:** Is only the listed concept taught as new, with all other doctrine retrieved rather than newly explained?
3. **Metanarrative link:** Does the story explicitly recall its prerequisite and move the promise–kingdom–rescue–presence story toward or outward from Christ?
4. **Gospel integrity:** Is God's action primary? Are grace and faith clear? Is obedience presented as fruit rather than purchase?
5. **Christ connection:** For the Old Testament, is the connection based on promise, covenant, pattern, office, or direct fulfilment—not a fanciful resemblance?
6. **Child safety:** Is difficult content truthful, calm, non-graphic, non-manipulative, and paired with permission to seek a trusted adult?
7. **Age fit:** Are sentences concrete and brief, symbols explained from prior stories, and application limited to one observable response?
8. **Memory and retrieval:** Is the assigned verse practised in context, and does the session retrieve at least one earlier core truth?
9. **Global belonging:** Do names, art, examples, and applications dignify African children and the worldwide, multiethnic church without stereotyping?
10. **Response:** Does the ending invite wonder, prayer, repentance, faith, or Spirit-enabled action appropriate to the doctrine—without manufacturing emotion or assurance?
